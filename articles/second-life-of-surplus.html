The Second Life of a Hotel Mirror

How things UK businesses were about to throw away end up helping soldiers learn to walk again, keeping medicine cold and getting kids back into PE.

By the Bridge2Ukraine team 5 min read

Picture a hotel halfway through a refurbishment. New carpets are going down, new furniture is arriving, and in the car park there's a skip full of perfectly good mirrors, bedside lamps, mini fridges and room safes. A few years ago, most of that would have gone straight to landfill.

These days, quite a lot of it goes to Ukraine instead.

A mirror, a fridge and a safe

Some of our favourite donations are the ones nobody expects to matter.

Mirrors from hotel rooms now hang in rehabilitation centres, where soldiers who've lost limbs use them to watch their posture as they learn to walk again. One physiotherapist told us they'd been trying to find full-length mirrors for months.

Mini fridges that used to hold overpriced chocolate now store insulin and other medicines in evacuation centres, where the power can go off at any moment.

Room safes give soldiers somewhere secure to keep documents and personal belongings, a small bit of privacy in a place that has very little.

"We had no idea our old mirrors could help someone walk again. It changed how our whole team thinks about clear-outs." Facilities manager, London hotel group (placeholder quote)

From the gym floor to the school hall

When a UK gym upgrades its equipment, the old kit still has years of life left. Weights, benches and mats donated by David Lloyd went to a school in the Kyiv region and to rehabilitation centres for injured soldiers. For the kids, it meant PE lessons could start again. For the soldiers, it meant they could do their recovery exercises properly.

What happens when a business calls us

We try to make donating as easy as throwing things away. Easier, ideally. Here's how it usually works:

  1. A quick chatYou tell us what you've got and when it needs to go. We check it against what our partners in Ukraine need right now.
  2. We collectOur volunteers come to you with a van, or a larger vehicle for big clearances. You don't need to sort or pack anything.
  3. We sort, pack and shipYour items go to our South England hub, onto pallets and onto the next lorry.
  4. You get the paperworkWe send you a green certificate showing how many tonnes you've kept out of landfill, which you can use in your ESG reporting.
  5. You see where it wentWhere we can, we send photos of your donation arriving in Ukraine. Lots of businesses share these with their staff.

Good for Ukraine, good for business

We won't pretend there's nothing in it for donors. Businesses can deduct the value of donated goods from their profits before corporation tax. They also avoid disposal costs and get verified figures for their sustainability reports. One partner told us donating saved them around £3,000 in skip hire on a single refurbishment.

But the main reason most businesses come back is simpler than that. It feels good to know your old stuff is doing something useful.

Things we're often looking for

  • Hotel furniture, mirrors, mini fridges, safes, bedding and towels
  • Medical supplies and equipment (in date and unopened)
  • Gym and rehabilitation equipment
  • School furniture, books and stationery
  • Warm clothing, especially before winter

Not sure if something's useful? Just ask. If we can't use it, we'll tell you.

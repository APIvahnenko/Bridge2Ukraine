Saturdays in Croydon

Tape guns, tea and a lot of cardboard. Meet the volunteers who turn a warehouse full of donations into aid that's ready for the road.

By the Bridge2Ukraine team 5 min read

It's half past eight on a Saturday morning in Croydon. The shutter goes up, the kettle goes on, and there's a mountain of donations waiting: bin bags of coats, boxes of medical supplies, a stack of hotel mirrors and, for some reason, a single rowing machine.

By the end of the day, all of it will be sorted, checked, packed, labelled and stacked on pallets, ready for the next lorry to Ukraine. None of the people doing it are paid.

Who turns up?

Honestly, all sorts. British and Ukrainian volunteers, working side by side. Everyone gives what they can, and that looks different for each of us.

The ones who treat it like a full-time job

Sacha, Tony and Zina are the heart of it all. Sacha is the person who makes everything happen. Tony is in the warehouse putting pallets together, sometimes seven days a week. Zina came to the UK as a refugee from Kyiv. She looks after everyone and always manages to cheer us up. None of them are paid.

The ones who spread the word

Not all of our volunteers lift boxes. Hazel, Bex, Stef, Kyla and Anna help tell people what we do. They share photos and stories, look after our social media and write pieces like this one. Kyla does all of it from South Africa, so you really can help from anywhere.

The evening and Saturday sorters

Then there are the people who come in after work or on a Saturday to sort donations. Some are friends who turn up whether it's freezing cold or the hottest day of summer. Some made a donation, saw the difference it made and now come to help too. There are British Ukrainians who've lived here for decades and want to do something to help. University students come to give their time, and teams from local businesses swap an awayday for a day in the warehouse. And there are people who spotted a Facebook post, came along once and are somehow still here years later.

Loading day

Every so often there's a big loading or unloading day. Sacha sends a message asking who can make it, and whoever's free turns up. These days don't come round often, but we love them. It's a lot of hard work, but it's so nice to see that lorry packed and ready to go.

Some people come once. Lots of people come back.

"I came for one Saturday to donate items. That was three years ago. I'm still here because you can see exactly where the work goes." Collin

The jobs nobody sees

Packing boxes is the bit that makes it into photos. But there's a lot more to a shift than that:

  1. SortingDonations arrive mixed up. Everything gets split by type: adult clothes, children's clothes, medical, hygiene, food, equipment.
  2. CheckingZips work, nothing's stained, medicines and food are in date. If it isn't good enough to give to someone you love, it doesn't go.
  3. LabellingEvery box is labelled in English and Ukrainian so our partners can get it to the right place fast. Our Ukrainian volunteers are brilliant at this.
  4. CountingEach box goes on the packing list for customs, line by line. It's not exciting, but without it the lorry doesn't cross the border.
  5. LiftingPallets, boxes, the odd rowing machine. Tony, who you might have seen in our photos, can move a pallet faster than anyone.

And outside the warehouse there's more: WhatsApp messages with partners in Ukraine at midnight, collection runs across the South East, and spreadsheets. So many spreadsheets.

Meanwhile, in Kyiv…

Our Croydon volunteers have a mirror image in Ukraine. When a lorry arrives, volunteers from our partner charities unload it by hand, often in the cold and sometimes between air raid sirens. Then they sort it all over again and drive it to the people who need it.

It's one long chain of people, starting with someone dropping off a bag of coats in South London and ending with a family in Sumy getting through the winter. Every link matters.
